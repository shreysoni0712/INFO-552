Appointment Help

Preparing for your medical appointment can help you get the most out of your visit. Here's what you need to know:

What to Bring

Identification

Photo ID (driver's license, state ID, or passport)

Medical Records

Previous test results, medical history, or referral letters

Current Medications

List of all medications you're taking, including dosages

Insurance Information

Insurance cards or proof of income for sliding scale fees

Questions to Ask Your Doctor

  • What is my diagnosis and what does it mean?
  • What treatment options are available?
  • Are there any side effects I should watch for?
  • When should I schedule a follow-up appointment?
  • Are there any lifestyle changes I should make?
  • Do I need any tests or screenings?

Tips for Your Visit

Arrive 15 minutes early to complete paperwork. Write down your questions beforehand so you don't forget them. Don't be afraid to ask for clarification if you don't understand something. Consider bringing a trusted friend or family member for support.

Low-Cost Medications

Finding affordable medications can be challenging. Here are resources to help you save money on prescriptions:

Prescription Assistance Programs

GoodRx

Free app and website that provides discounts on prescription medications at participating pharmacies.

Pharmaceutical Company Programs

Many drug manufacturers offer patient assistance programs for their medications.

Clinic Pharmacies

Some community health centers have on-site pharmacies with reduced prices.

Local Pharmacy Resources

CVS Health Charitable Trust

Provides free health screenings and medication assistance for qualifying individuals.

1-800-SHOP-CVS

Rite Aid Foundation

Offers medication assistance programs and health services for underserved communities.

1-800-RITE-AID

Generic Medication Options

Ask your doctor or pharmacist about generic versions of your medications. Generic drugs contain the same active ingredients as brand-name drugs but cost significantly less. The FDA requires generic drugs to be just as safe and effective as brand-name medications.

Support groups can provide comfort, reduce feelings of isolation, and offer practical advice. Many groups are free and meet regularly at community centers, churches, and healthcare facilities throughout Philadelphia. Contact the organizations above to find groups that meet your specific needs.
